Common Symptoms of Brain Tumors

Nausea & Vomiting

Nausea and vomiting occur frequently due to increased pressure inside the skull caused by the tumor. This pressure affects the brain's vomiting center, leading to persistent feelings of sickness and vomiting, which may worsen in the morning or after sudden head movements.

Weakness or Numbness in Limbs

Tumors can press on nerves or areas of the brain controlling muscle movement and sensation. This results in weakness or numbness in the arms or legs, often on one side of the body, making it difficult to perform normal activities or maintain balance.

Speech Difficulties

When the tumor affects language centers in the brain, patients may experience trouble forming words, slurred speech, or difficulty understanding others. This symptom often signals that the tumor is located near areas responsible for communication.

Personality or Memory Changes

Brain tumors can impact parts of the brain responsible for emotions, behavior, and memory. Patients may show noticeable personality changes, mood swings, confusion, or memory loss, affecting daily life and relationships.
